Interrupt Masking

Interrupt masking allows you to disable the detection of an interrupt line assertion, which causes the interrupt signal to be ignored. This can happen at the microprocessor or other levels in the architecture of the hardware.

In some cases, each interrupt source in the system can be masked individually. In other cases, masking an interrupt in a microprocessor register can mask a group of interrupts. An example of this is shared interrupts.

When an interrupt occurs, the microprocessor needs to globally disable interrupts at the microprocessor in order to avoid being interrupted while gathering and saving interrupt state information. In your OAL ISR code, leave global interrupts masked for as small amount of time as possible because you are blocking all other interrupts. When you determine what is specifically interrupting, you can mask just that interrupt.
