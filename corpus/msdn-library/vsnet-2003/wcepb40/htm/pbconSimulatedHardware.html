Emulated Hardware

The Emulator allows you to emulate various pieces of hardware that you may find on a physical hardware platform. You can choose specific pieces of hardware to emulate in your platform before you download an OS image to the Emulator. For more information, see Downloading an OS Image to the Emulator.

The Emulator provides a parallel port that you can map directly to an LPT port on your development workstation. The Emulator also provides two serial ports, or virtual COM ports, that you can map to communications (COM) ports on your development workstation.

If you include support for KITL in your OS image, the OS sends its output to Serial Port 1. If the OS supports KITL, use Serial Port 2 as a product serial port. With a retail OS image, you may be able to use both Serial Port 1 and Serial Port 2 as product serial ports.

With the Emulator, you can choose to enable Ethernet support. If you enable Ethernet support, the Emulator supports a single Ethernet card using shared Internet Protocol (IP). You must use an Ethernet card that can serve as the Ethernet card for a physical Windows CE–based target device. The Emulator uses the Ethernet card on your development workstation. The Emulator emulates a DEC 21040 Ethernet card, but the card on the development workstation is not required to have a driver for Windows CE.

You can specify a size for the Emulator display that is as small as 80 by 64 pixels or as large as 1024 by 768 pixels. You can specify a color depth of 8, 16, or 32 bits per pixel.

The Emulator supports a standard keyboard and a PS/2 mouse. For information about keyboard support for the Emulator, see Emulator Key Combinations. The Emulator also provides basic support for audio, including audio input, audio output, microphone input, and line output.
