VoIP Performance

Properly handling samples in the audio driver is a key element to achieving good sound quality and performance with voice over IP (VoIP). Your driver's audio capture characteristics will have a greater influence on overall VoIP quality than your driver's audio rendering characteristics. These characteristics can have an effect on performance that is more important than, and even independent of, your device's raw processing power.

The smallest captured sound sample used in the VoIP system is about 20 milliseconds (ms) long. The actual sample size will depend upon the audio codec being used. It is set in the sample's WAVEHDR structure. Some audio hardware will generate an interrupt for variable length captures. If your hardware does not support this, then you should simulate this behavior in your device driver. Your audio driver should send an interrupt as soon as each sample is complete and the WAVEHDR structure is filled. This interrupt will cause the captured audio data to be sent immediately to the encoder and then on to the receiving device.

If your driver does not send an interrupt at the end of every sample, then the samples will accumulate in the DMA buffer until it is full. Once the DMA buffer is full, the audio hardware will create an interrupt and send all of the samples down the network in one large stream. This can lead to audio that is received and rendered in bursts because, depending on the specific hardware configuration, the DMA buffer can hold as much as 200ms worth of audio data.

The overall latency for a VoIP receiving device is the sum of the times spent capturing the audio data, encoding it, sending it over the network, decoding it, and finally rendering it. If the audio capture time is the sum of many samples filling the entire DMA buffer then the total end-to-end time of the entire process can exceed the acceptable latency limit for a phone call, which is about 200ms. You can achieve low-latency audio capture by indicating the completion of small WAVEHDR structures as soon as each one is filled.
