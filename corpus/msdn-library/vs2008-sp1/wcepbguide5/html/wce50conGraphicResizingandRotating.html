Graphic Resizing and Rotating

The behavior of the graphics editor while resizing a graphic depends on whether the selection includes the entire graphic or just part of it.

When the selection includes only part of the graphic, the graphics editor makes the selection smaller by deleting rows or columns of pixels and filling the blank areas with the current background color. It makes the selection larger by duplicating rows or columns of pixels.

If the selection includes the entire graphic, you can use the graphics editor to resize the overall graphic area or the image within it in the following ways:

  • When you make the overall graphic area larger, the graphics editor extends that area to the right, down, or both, and fills the new area with the current background color. The graphic image within the graphic area is not stretched to fill the area.
  • In contrast, when you make the overall graphic area smaller, the graphics editor crops the graphic image on the right or bottom edge, or both. The cropped portion of the image is permanently lost.
  • When you resize the graphic image that is within the overall graphic area, pixels are removed from or added to the image to retain its proportions as you change its size.

You drag the sizing handles to change the size of all or part of a graphic. Sizing handles that you can drag are solid, and ones that you cannot drag are hollow. You can use the property page to resize the overall graphic area, but not just part of it.

If you have the Tile Grid check box selected in Grid Settings from the Image menu, resizing snaps the graphic to the next tile gridline. If only the Pixel Grid check box is selected, resizing snaps the graphic to the next available pixel. Usually, only the Pixel Grid check box is selected.
