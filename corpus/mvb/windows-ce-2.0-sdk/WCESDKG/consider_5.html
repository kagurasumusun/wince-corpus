User Interface Considerations

Some of the greatest differences between Windows CE devices and a standard desktop are in the user-interface. Though some Windows CE devices, such as the H/PC, may be similar to a conventional PC, others may bear little resemblance. The familiar screen, keyboard, and mouse of a desktop UI may be modified or completely lacking on a Windows CE device.

For Windows CE devices, the screen is not as powerful and flexible a way to communicate information to the user as it is with a PC because. the screen is typically much smaller, with lower resolution. Though some devices have color screens, many support only greyscale or monochrome graphics. For devices that dispense with the screen entirely, you may need to develop alternative ways to communicate information.

A key element of a graphical UI is a pointing device, which allows a user to interact with the various graphical elements of the UI. However, the conventional mouse may not be available on Windows CE devices. Some devices, such as the H/PC, have a touch screen, which works in much the same fashion. Others may have no pointing device at all. The latter may depend on primitive navigation techniques, such as arrow keys, or may use an entirely different approach to interaction, such as speech recognition.

Keyboards on Windows CE devices will generally be less flexible and easy to use than they are on desktop PCs. They may not be large enough to support a full alphanumeric keyboard, and therefore they may only provide a collection of special-purpose keys for users to communicate the application. Text-entry, if supported, may be through a soft-input panel.Since this method of text entry is more difficult for users, applications will need to minimize the amount needed and use other forms of communication whenever possible.
