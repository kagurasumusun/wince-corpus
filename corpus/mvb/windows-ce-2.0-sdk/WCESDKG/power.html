Managing Power Consumption

A Handheld PC (H/PC) must actively manage its power consumption to extend the life of its limited batteries. As part of its power management strategy, the operating system automatically selects operating states to conserve power based on user and program activities.

All applications can contribute to power management by returning control to the operating system when possible to let it determine when the system is inactive. It can then select the most efficient operating state for the device. Most applications do not need any special techniques for effective power management; they can let the operating system manage the power.

Time-based applications, or applications that are affected if their execution is suspended are most affected by power management activities. The subtopics in this section describe how the different device operating states might affect some applications and what an application can do for efficient operation under the different power management operating modes.

Primarily, the function of power management is to increase the battery life of the computer. Power management processes also provide accurate estimates of remaining battery life and notify the user that the batteries are nearing depletion.

In increasing battery life, power management makes the following assumptions:

  • Typical use is less than two hours per day in bursts from five minutes to one hour long.

  • The display is on 100% of the time during use.

  • The CPU is on less than 10% of the time during typical use.

  • The computer uses both main batteries and a backup battery.

  • The device has no nonvolatile writable memory.

Maximum battery life is obtained without PC Cards or with PC Cards that require very little power from the computer batteries. PC Cards drawing appreciable power from the internal batteries significantly reduce battery life.
