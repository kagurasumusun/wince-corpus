4.1 PR3910 Registers

There are 32 general registers, each consisting of a single word (32 bits). The 32 general registers are treated symmetrically, with two exceptions: register 0 (r0) is hard wired to a zero value, and register 31 (r31) is the link register for Jump And Link instructions.

Register r0 may be specified as a target register for any instruction when the result of the operation is discarded. The register maintains a value of zero under all conditions when used as a source register.

The two Multiply/Divide registers (HI, LO) store the double-word, 64-bit result of multiply operations and the quotient and remainder of divide operations.

In addition to the CPU’s general registers, the system control coprocessor (CP0) has a number of special purpose registers that are used during exception processing and to configure certain processor and cache options.
