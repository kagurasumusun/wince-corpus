Method: the PDF's text streams were inflated (zlib) and the text-showing
operators concatenated, then whitespace folded.  The scan is an OCR-derived
image PDF, so a small number of glyphs are misrecognised in the original
("reproducec;l" for "reproduced", "MiCrosoft" for "Microsoft"); those are left
as the extraction produced them and are not silently corrected.  Transcribing
the page here is what lets data/license-scopes.tsv quote the statement and
tools/check-licenses.py verify the quote: the checker reads HTML and text, not
PDF, so the wording a reader sees on the copyright page is kept as a text file
next to the PDF it was read from — the same convention as
sources/terms/learn-com-termsofuse-excerpt.txt.

Why this matters for this project: these are **commercially published books**
(Microsoft Press, with ISBNs, Library of Congress cataloguing and a retail
ordering channel), not free product documentation.  queues/third-party-sources.md
records the collection rule that was applied to the same bitsavers directory and
concluded that these files "stay uncollected"; sources/bitsavers-microsoft/
PROVENANCE.md reaches the opposite conclusion and the files are present.  Both
cannot stand — see docs/review-rights-2026-10.ja.md §5.
